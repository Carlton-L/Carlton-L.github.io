# Design Philosophy & Approach

## Make Things That Work

My core principle is building things that actually work — not just concepts or mockups, but functional prototypes and shipped products. I come from hardware engineering where something either works or it doesn't. That pragmatism carries into my design and software work.

## Bridge Hardware and Software

I sit at an unusual intersection. Most designers don't solder PCBs. Most hardware engineers don't write React. I value this cross-disciplinary perspective because the best products come from understanding the full stack — from the physical materials to the user interface.

## Prototype First, Polish Later

I believe in getting something working as fast as possible, then iterating. A rough prototype that you can test teaches you more than a polished mockup that you can't. This is why I gravitate toward rapid prototyping tools and frameworks that minimize boilerplate.

## Research-Informed, Not Research-Paralyzed

My Master's in Interaction Design gave me strong research skills — user interviews, contextual inquiry, design ethnography. But I've learned that research is a means, not an end. The goal is always to build something better, not to produce more research documents.

## AI as a Tool, Not a Gimmick

I integrate AI where it genuinely adds value — like using LLMs for futures scenario generation in FAST, or building this digital twin chatbot. I don't add AI to projects just because it's trendy. The question is always: does this make the experience better for the user?

## Systems Thinking

I think about how pieces connect. A smart home lighting system isn't just code — it's sensors, algorithms, physical space, and human behavior interacting. I design for these interactions, not just individual components.
